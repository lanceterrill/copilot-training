Why you're taking this training

HIPAA requires it twice over. The Privacy Rule (45 CFR §164.530(b)) requires training every member of the workforce on our PHI policies and procedures. The Security Rule (45 CFR §164.308(a)(5)) separately requires a security awareness and training program for all workforce members, management included. "Workforce" is broad: employees, volunteers, trainees, interns, temporary staff, and on-site contractors. New staff train within a reasonable period after joining; everyone retrains when policies materially change — and adopting AI tools is exactly such a change, which is why this site exists.

What counts as PHI

PHI is health information that identifies a person (or reasonably could), created or received in the course of care, payment, or operations. It covers all formats: charts, spreadsheets, emails, chat messages, meeting transcripts, voicemails, photos. For a record to be de-identified under the Safe Harbor method, all 18 identifiers must be gone:

  • 1. Names — patient, family members, providers
  • 2. Geographic units smaller than a state (street, city, county, most ZIP digits)
  • 3. All dates except year — birth, admission, discharge, service, death (and ages over 89 aggregated to "90+")
  • 4. Telephone numbers
  • 5. Fax numbers
  • 6. Email addresses
  • 7. Social Security numbers
  • 8. Medical record numbers
  • 9. Health plan beneficiary numbers
  • 10. Account numbers
  • 11. Certificate or license numbers
  • 12. Vehicle identifiers, including license plates
  • 13. Device identifiers and serial numbers
  • 14. Web URLs tied to a person
  • 15. IP addresses
  • 16. Biometric identifiers (fingerprints, voiceprints)
  • 17. Full-face photos and comparable images
  • 18. Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code
PHI rule · combinations identify

Removing the obvious identifiers is not enough if what remains still points to one person — a rare diagnosis plus an age plus a small town can identify someone as surely as a name. When judging whether something is de-identified, ask whether the combination could identify, not just each field alone.

Core Privacy Rule habits

  • Minimum necessary — use and disclose only what the task requires (treatment itself is the main exception; providers may need the full record).
  • Role-based access — your access matches your job. Don't look up patients out of curiosity, including family, coworkers, or public figures.
  • Authorizations — uses beyond treatment, payment, and operations generally need the patient's written authorization.
  • Safeguards — lock screens, secure documents, speak about patients where you can't be overheard.

Breach basics — what happens after you report

When an unauthorized use or disclosure occurs, the organization assesses it, and if it's a breach: affected individuals are notified within 60 days; breaches affecting 500 or more people are also reported to HHS within 60 days and to the media; smaller breaches are logged and reported to HHS annually. Your job in this process is the front end — report the same day, and never conceal or delete anything. Concealment converts a correctable mistake into a serious violation.

Before you take the quiz, you should be able to

  • Say which two rules require this training and who counts as "workforce."
  • Recognize the 18 identifiers and the combination principle.
  • Describe minimum necessary and the curiosity-access rule.
  • State your reporting duty and the 60-day notification frame that follows.
