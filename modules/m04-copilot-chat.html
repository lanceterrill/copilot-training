Copilot Chat

≈ 25 minutes · complete M-01 first

Two grounding modes — know which one you're in

  • Work — answers draw on your organization's Microsoft 365 content (your files, mail, meetings). Use this for "what did we decide about X?" or "summarize the drafts in my folder."
  • Web — answers draw on public web content. Use this for general knowledge, current events, and how-to questions.

The toggle sits at the top of the chat. Checking it before you type is a habit worth building — it changes both what Copilot can see and where your question travels.

Prompts that work

A good prompt has four parts: the goal (what you want), the context (why, for whom), the source (which files or threads to use), and the expectations (format, length, tone). Compare:

  • Weak: "summarize this."
  • Strong: "Summarize this policy draft in five bullets for front-desk staff who have two minutes. Plain language, no jargon."

Iterate. Treat the first answer as a first draft and reply with corrections — "shorter," "add the deadline," "use the numbers from the table only."

Verify before you rely

  • Copilot cites the files and pages it used — open the citations for anything that matters.
  • AI systems can state wrong things fluently. Numbers, dates, names, and policy claims get checked against the source, every time.
  • If an answer will influence a decision about a patient, a payment, or a policy, a human verifies it first. No exceptions.
PHI rule

Only enter PHI in tools our organization has approved for PHI — that means the signed-in, work version of Copilot inside our tenant, and only when your task requires it. Never put patient information into a personal AI account, a free consumer chatbot, or any tool you merely assume is covered. If you're not certain a tool is approved, it isn't — ask first.
